# Code of Conduct

Ferrite contributors and maintainers must help keep project spaces safe, useful, and respectful.

## Expected conduct

- Discuss the work, not a person's identity or worth.
- Give specific, technical, and actionable feedback.
- Accept correction and revise claims when evidence changes.
- Respect names, pronouns, privacy, and personal boundaries.
- Assume no right to another person's time, access, or private information.
- Keep security reports and private project information in the proper private channel.
- Disclose conflicts of interest that could affect a review or decision.

## Unacceptable conduct

- Harassment, threats, stalking, intimidation, or sustained personal attacks.
- Discrimination or degrading remarks based on identity, background, disability, health, religion, or other personal traits.
- Sexual attention or content directed at another participant without consent.
- Doxxing, exposing private communications, or sharing personal data without permission.
- Deliberate disruption, spam, bad-faith review, or repeated refusal to follow maintainer direction.
- Publishing vulnerability details before the maintainer has had a fair chance to assess and fix them.
- Retaliation against a person who reports a concern in good faith.

## Scope

This policy applies to repository issues, pull requests, reviews, discussions, project chat, events, and private communication tied to Ferrite. It also applies when someone publicly represents the project.

## Reporting

Report conduct concerns privately to the repository owner through GitHub. Do not post sensitive details in a public issue. Include links, dates, screenshots, or other direct evidence when available.

The maintainer may remove content, restrict participation, close work, block accounts, or refer serious matters to the relevant platform. Decisions should account for context, impact, prior conduct, and the safety of affected people.

Good-faith reports will be handled as privately as practical. This policy does not promise secrecy when disclosure is needed to protect someone, comply with law, or enforce platform rules.
